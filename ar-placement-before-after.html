Little Buddy · AR placement improvements · November 3, 2026

A steadier place
for Little Buddy.

Stronger surface checks, more accurate placement geometry, and safer recovery when the real world changes.

Implemented & locally testedAndroid + iOSReal-device visual review pending

The main improvement

The reader is more careful about when it places Buddy and whether there is still enough measured surface beneath the character afterward.

This is a placement and grounding update—not a new character model, animation system, or proven photorealistic rendering upgrade. Most changes close gaps in iOS; Android already had several of these safeguards.

Before → After

What the code did before, what it does now, and why that matters.

01

Confirming a real surface

iOS
Before

Automatic placement waited for a steady measured hit, but manual placement could bypass that wait. There was no camera-parallax requirement.

After

Both manual and automatic placement require a stable target plus at least 8 cm of sideways camera movement and 1.5 seconds of stable surface-height observation.

Why it helps: reduces premature placement on a surface that AR is still reconstructing. Guidance now asks the user to move slowly sideways, then hold steady.
02

Checking enough room for Buddy

iOS
Before

The automatic flow checked the plane’s rectangular extent using a fixed 25 cm × user scale margin. Manual placement did not use that same room check.

After

Every placement checks the actual measured plane boundary, using a rotation-independent footprint calculated from the normalized model, with an extra 1 cm margin.

Why it helps: rejects unobserved corners inside a plane’s bounding rectangle, while avoiding an unnecessarily large, fixed room requirement.
03

Matching the preview to the loaded model

iOS · Little Buddy
Before

Preview dimensions were measured relative to the USDZ’s loaded root. That excludes the root’s Z-up → Y-up correction, so preview bounds did not match the rendered presentation basis.

After

An identity wrapper includes the loader’s existing correction when measuring bounds. No extra rotation is applied. Native loading confirms a Y-up height of approximately 20.6 cm before the reader’s 35 cm normalization.

Why it helps: footprint and preview dimensions now use the same upright geometry as the placed model. This fixes a bounds mismatch—not a demonstrated sideways-rendering bug.
04

Keeping the whole figure supported

Android + iOS
Before

Android checked the placed anchor’s point and height against its supporting plane. iOS mainly relied on normal camera tracking and whether the committed entity was anchored.

After

Both readers recheck support beneath the full footprint. iOS also verifies foot height within 3 cm of the plane and accepts a valid merged plane. Sustained loss hides the unreliable figure and pauses playback.

Why it helps: a tracked anchor alone no longer counts as proof that Buddy still has a suitable surface underneath it.
05

Trusting fresh camera evidence

Android + iOS
Before

Repeated timestamps could increase the settling sample count. On iOS, display updates could count the same camera frame repeatedly, and camera tracking had no explicit frame-age check.

After

A camera timestamp counts only once toward the settling sample count. iOS uses the camera frame’s timestamp for dwell and rejects tracking frames older than 250 milliseconds.

Why it helps: a fast display refresh or stalled camera cannot manufacture new surface observations.
06

Making Move predictable

iOS
Before

In the automatic scan flow, a steady target could confirm a Move automatically. Moving also recalculated the viewer-facing heading, changing Buddy’s established world orientation.

After

Automatic placement applies only to the initial search. Move requires explicit confirmation and preserves the existing world heading, user rotation, and size. Recreated previews retain the current adjustment.

Why it helps: moving Buddy changes its location without unexpectedly confirming the action or turning the character.
07

Handling unreliable placement safely

iOS
Before

Audio and adjustment gates did not verify measured surface support. The model could remain visible during recovery even when its placement was no longer reliable.

After

Starting audio and adjusting Buddy require healthy placement. Brief loss is debounced; sustained loss hides the model and pauses narration. Recovery does not automatically resume paused narration.

Why it helps: avoids continuing an apparently normal reading experience while the spatial placement is untrustworthy.

What passed locally

  • Android release Kotlin compilation: passed.
  • Real bundled USDZ loading: passed in iOS Simulator. Tests check upright dimensions and bottom-contact math at 0.5×, 1×, and 1.5× user scale.
  • Regression coverage: repeated frames, slow drift, camera parallax, plane changes, polygon edges, reachability, and loader-root transforms.
  • Model audit: Android asset copies match the source. All eight GLB materials use lit PBR shading, without an unlit extension.

What is not proven yet

These changes improve the conditions for believable AR. They do not prove that Buddy looks realistic on a phone.

  • Environmental HDR, shadow receiving, and RealityKit environment texturing already existed; they were not introduced by this update.
  • Depth/people occlusion experiments remain off by default. Objects and people hiding Buddy correctly still need physical testing.
  • Little Buddy remains a static prototype: no new idle animation, talking rig, lip sync, or photorealistic texture work.
  • Surface-finding success, real shadows, contact, facing, drift, recovery, and sustained performance need an unlocked, trusted AR-capable device. None is currently connected.
